StudioLAB Growth, onboarding form

What the form promises, and what actually happens

An accuracy sweep of every claim the onboarding form makes to a studio, checked against what the code, the database and the post-payment surfaces actually do. Voice comes later. Every defect below is friendly, well written, and would survive a tone pass untouched.

4 Untrue We tell a studio something that is not so
6 Contradictory Two of our own surfaces disagree
4 Unreachable Copy or logic no studio can get to
14 Total None fixed yet, all verified in code

Swept: the six live routes (au and us × Launch, Scale, Dominate AI), account.html, js/form.js, and the save-draft, save-kb, scrape-and-extract and cleanup-attachments functions. Not swept: kb.html, the admin console, and outbound email templates.

Untrue

4 findings

A studio reads these and forms a false expectation. Two of them also change what data we collect, which makes them more than a copy problem.

U1

We promise to handle email DNS, which we cannot do without access to their registrar

All six routes, step 3, two separate places each. 12 instances.

What the studio reads

Sending from your own domain helps your emails land in the inbox instead of the spam folder. If you would like this, just tell us the domain and we set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for you during onboarding. There is nothing technical for you to do.

Just the domain, not a full URL. We handle all the technical setup during onboarding, so you will not need to touch any DNS records yourself.

au/scale/index.html lines 527 and 540, mirrored on all six routes

What is actually so

SPF, DKIM and DMARC are records on the studio's own domain, held at their registrar. We can generate the values, but somebody with access to that DNS has to publish them. We do not have that access and the form never asks for it.

The form even has a dns_access question, so the product already knows this varies by studio. The copy promises the outcome anyway.

The second cost is worse than the false promise. A studio who has no idea how to reach their DNS is exactly the studio who should be steered to Done For You. This line tells them there is nothing to worry about, so that signal never fires.

Fix

Say what we do and what they do: we send the exact records and walk them through it, or on Done For You we do it with them on a call. Then let the answer route them.

U2

We require the last four of a US owner's social security number on a plan that has no texting

us/launch. Verified live in the browser: the field renders and carries data-required.

What the studio reads

Last 4 of owner SSN
Required by US carriers to register a Sole Proprietor SMS sender. We never see the full number.

Shown on us/launch as soon as a studio selects Sole Proprietor, under a heading that says these details are optional

What is actually so

Launch has no SMS. There is no carrier registration, no texting number, and nothing for those digits to be used for.

applyBusinessTypeConditionals() in js/form.js decides visibility from country and business type only. It never checks the plan. So the field appears on Launch exactly as it does on Scale, and because it also carries data-required, a US sole proprietor cannot get past step 2 without entering it.

This is a data question before it is a copy question: we would be collecting sensitive identity data for a stated purpose that does not exist on that plan, and refusing to continue without it.

Fix

Gate the SSN and EIN fields on the plan as well as the country, so they only appear where texting is actually included. This one is worth flagging to whoever advises you on US privacy, since it touches what we hold and why.

U3

The consent tick box asks Launch studios to authorise text messages we will never send

au/launch and us/launch, step 4. This is the box that blocks submission.

What the studio reads

I authorise StudioLAB Growth to send emails and text messages to my families on my studio's behalf, using the studio name, branding, and contact details I have provided, for the automations and communications included in my plan.

Identical wording on all six routes, including both Launch routes

What is actually so

Launch is email automations only. The authorisation collected is broader than the service bought.

The clause does end with "included in my plan", which limits it, but the studio is still being asked to tick a permission for a channel they have not purchased. On a page whose whole job is to be trustworthy about what happens to their families' contact details, that reads badly.

Elsewhere the form already handles this correctly. The business details intro says the tax details matter "on text-enabled plans", which is exactly the right hedge. The consent line simply was not given the same treatment.

Fix

Drop "and text messages" on the Launch routes. Same consent version, same audit trail, one clause narrower. Worth a second opinion from your legal adviser given it is a recorded authorisation.

U4

We say texting needs nothing technical from them, then the account page hands them a technical task list

Scale and Dominate AI, step 4, against the SMS task in account.html.

What the studio reads, before paying

If you would like it, we set up your texting number and handle the carrier registration for you during onboarding. You do not need a provider or any technical setup.

au/scale and au/ai, step 4. The US routes add "(A2P / 10DLC)" but keep the same promise.

What the same studio reads after paying

On Done For You they still have to pick an industry vertical, describe how families opt in, review two sample messages, and supply a Privacy Policy URL and a Terms of Service URL, both of which have to mention SMS handling and STOP or HELP.

On Guided it goes further: "Publish a Privacy Policy and Terms of Service on your website", then "Inside StudioLAB Growth, go to Settings, Phone Numbers, A2P Registration and submit your business identity."

Both lists live in account.html under the sms_a2p task, so this is our own documented process contradicting our own pre-sale copy.

Fix

Say we handle the number, the carrier paperwork and the submission, and that they will need a privacy policy and terms page on their site. Naming it before payment also gives Done For You a real reason to exist.

Contradictory

6 findings

Nothing here is a lie on its own. Each one is two StudioLAB surfaces telling the same studio different things, usually minutes apart.

C1

Every route disagrees with itself about how long the form takes

All six routes. The two numbers sit one screen apart.

The two screens, in order

Launch
Welcome door: About 5 minutes
Step 1 card: Takes about 10 minutes

Scale
Welcome door: About 10 minutes
Step 1 card: Takes about 15 minutes

Dominate AI
Welcome door: About 15 minutes
Step 1 card: This form takes about 10 minutes

What is actually so

There is one coded source of truth: PLAN_MINS in js/setup-gate.js holds 5, 10 and 15. The old auth gate used it, and the new welcome door inherited it. The step 1 cards are hand written text that nobody reconciled.

Dominate AI is the one that stings. The door says 15 minutes and the very next screen says 10, so the longest plan advertises itself as the quickest. It also happens to be the plan with the highest setup fee.

Fix

Treat PLAN_MINS as canon and rewrite the three step 1 cards to match. Better still, render the number from that constant so the two can never drift again.

C2

Three surfaces quote four different turnaround times for the same setup

Step 1, the setup cards at step 5, and account.html after payment.

What a Scale studio reads, in sequence

Step 1
Your account is typically ready in 3 to 7 business days

Step 5, the card they pay on
Done For You: Typically 5 to 7 business days
Guided: Typically 3 to 5 business days

account.html, right after paying
Reviewing your submission and setting up your account behind the scenes. This typically takes 1 to 2 business days

What is actually so

The setup card text is the one generated from code (SETUP_DISPLAY in js/form.js), and it is the number attached to the price they agree to, so it is the one that counts commercially.

The account page then quotes 1 to 2 days to the same studio, which is both the lowest number and the last one they read. Beating it looks impossible; missing it looks like a broken promise on the surface they will check repeatedly.

Dominate AI is consistent, at 7 to 10 days in both places. Only the Launch and Scale branch is wrong.

Fix

Pick one number per plan and setup type, put it in code once, and have step 1 and the account page both read from it. This is your call rather than mine, since it is a commitment to customers rather than a technical detail.

C3

We tell Scale studios to prepare their lead sources, then never ask for them

Scale and Dominate AI. Step 1 promises it, step 4 is titled after it, no input exists.

What the studio reads

Step 1, what you'll need before you start
Which lead sources to connect: Facebook Lead Ads, Google Business Messages, website chat

Step 4 heading and intro
Text messaging and lead sources
Optional setup for SMS automations and lead-source tracking on your Scale plan.

What is actually so

Step 4 collects one thing: a Google Business Profile URL. There is no lead source picker anywhere in any route.

collectLeads() reads input[data-lead]. There are zero such inputs across all six routes, so lead_sources is written as an empty array on every save and the handover document reaches the setup team blank.

So a studio does homework we asked for, arrives ready, is never asked, and we start their build without the answer.

Fix

Two honest options: add the picker back to step 4, or delete both promises and rename the step. Adding it is the better one, since the setup team genuinely needs the answer, but either beats the current state.

C4

The review summary promises we will activate everything, on the screen where they choose to do it themselves

All six routes, step 5, directly above the Done For You and Guided choice.

What the studio reads

Automations   Included
All Scale automations will be activated during setup

Static text, shown identically whichever setup path is selected

What is actually so

On Guided self setup the studio configures their own account from a checklist. We do not activate it for them, that is the entire distinction between the two paths and the reason they cost different amounts.

Sitting inches above the choice, this line quietly argues that the cheaper option gets the same hands on treatment. It undercuts the case for Done For You on the exact screen where that case has to be made.

Fix

Make the line follow the selection: "we activate all Scale automations for you" on Done For You, "your checklist covers all Scale automations" on Guided. The selection already drives other copy on that step, so the wiring exists.

C5

The US routes are written in Australian English

us/launch, us/scale, us/ai. Nine visible instances per route.

What a US studio reads

Brand colour hex codes, or a screenshot we can match

Brand colours  ·  Primary colour  ·  Secondary colour

Please choose a primary colour.

Drop a screenshot or website image here and we will match the colours for you.

Please enter your postcode.

What is actually so

There is a runtime spelling swap, swapSpelling(), and it looked like this was handled. It only converts the enrol and enrolment family. Colour, authorise, organise and the rest are untouched.

The word "authorise" was fixed by hand in the US consent text, which shows somebody noticed the problem and fixed one instance rather than the rule.

The brand voice canon we corrected in the last session says country aware surfaces use the reader's conventions. A route literally called /us/ is as country aware as it gets.

Also worth a look while you are in there: the ZIP field is labelled "Postcode / ZIP" with the placeholder "e.g. 3000 or 90210", offering an Australian example to an American studio.

Fix

Extend swapSpelling() to the full our/or and ise/ize sets rather than patching the six HTML files, so any copy added later is converted automatically. Then set the ZIP label and placeholder from the region.

C6

Both regions are offered the other country's legal entity types

All six routes, step 2, on the field that feeds tax and carrier registration.

The same list on every route

Sole Proprietor  ·  LLC  ·  Corporation  ·  Partnership  ·  Non-profit  ·  Pty Ltd (Australian company)  ·  Other Australian entity  ·  Other

Not filtered by region, so an Australian studio is offered LLC and a US studio is offered Pty Ltd

What is actually so

LLC is not an Australian structure and Pty Ltd is not a US one. An Australian owner scanning that list can reasonably pick LLC, thinking it is the generic company option.

The ABN and EIN fields underneath are driven by country rather than by this answer, so nothing breaks. But the stored business_type is then wrong on a record that feeds invoices and SMS sender registration.

Fix

Filter the option list by region, the same way the timezone list already is. Keep "Other" on both.

Unreachable

4 findings

No studio can see any of this today, so none of it is urgent. It matters because it makes the codebase lie to whoever reads it next, including me. I nearly filed the first one as a live contradiction before tracing who actually calls it.

D1

A finished success screen that only a spam bot has ever seen

The doneScreen block in all six routes.

What the file says a studio sees

All received. Thank you!
We've got everything we need to get your Scale setup underway. Our team will review your submission and be in touch shortly.

Timeline   Typically 3 to 5 business days

What is actually so

Two functions can reveal that panel. showDone() is called from exactly one place, the honeypot branch that fires when the hidden spam trap field is filled. showSavedForLater() overwrites the heading, the description and the timeline with "Draft saved. Pay when you're ready."

So the written copy reaches nobody. A real studio goes from step 5 to Stripe and comes back to the account page.

The risk is not today, it is the next person. Anyone reading these files reasonably believes studios see this, and the timelines in it disagree with the setup cards. The Dominate AI version quotes 5 to 10 days against a card that says 7 to 10.

Fix

Either delete the block, or leave it and put one comment at the top saying which two functions reach it and that both rewrite the text. A comment costs nothing and stops the next audit chasing the same ghost.

D2

A returning studio is never told their work was restored

js/form.js, enterForm().

What the code intends

if (isReturning) { const banner = document.getElementById('restoredBanner'); if (banner) banner.classList.add('vis'); }

What is actually so

No element with that id exists in any route. The lookup is guarded, so nothing errors, it simply never shows.

A studio who comes back days later, signs in and lands mid form gets no acknowledgement that anything was saved. The reassurance was designed and then lost.

Fix

Small and worth doing: add the banner. "Welcome back, we've restored everything you filled in." The door already promises the form saves as you go, so this is the moment that proves it.

D3

Validation that demands ten FAQs on a step that does not exist

js/form.js, validatePanel().

What the code intends

if (stepNum === 7 && state.plan === 'ai') { const faqPairs = collectFaqs(); if (faqPairs.length < 10) ok = false; }

What is actually so

The Dominate AI route has five panels. There is no step 7, and no #faqList element anywhere, so collectFaqs() would return nothing even if it ran.

This is a leftover from the rebuilt form that was shelved in July. It is the same family as the knowledge base fields that were nulling the scrape, which were live enough to destroy data.

Fix

Delete it, along with collectFaqs(), which now has no other caller.

D4

Every route has one unclosed div

All six routes. Predates the welcome door.

The count

au/scale: 328 opening tags, 327 closing
us/ai: 332 opening tags, 331 closing
Same pattern on all six

What is actually so

Browsers close it silently at the end of the body, which is why nothing looks wrong and why it has survived this long.

It is listed here because it makes structural checking unreliable. Any future audit has to work out whether the imbalance is the old one or a new one, which is exactly the sort of small ambiguity that hides a real break.

Fix

Find it once, close it in the generator, and the balance check becomes a clean pass or fail from then on.

Three things I need from you

Blocking pass 3 and 4

Everything else I can decide and build. These three are commitments to customers, so they are yours.

How long does setup actually take, per plan and per setup type?

Right now we say 1 to 2 days, 3 to 5, 3 to 7, 5 to 7 and 7 to 10 across different screens. Give me the real numbers you are willing to be held to and I will make every surface read from them.

Do we still want lead sources captured at onboarding?

The form promises to ask and then does not. Either I add the picker back to step 4, or I strip the promise and rename the step. Adding it is my recommendation, because the setup team needs the answer either way and is currently starting builds without it.

We cannot publish DNS records on a domain we have no access to. So what is the real offer: we send them the records and a guide, we do it live with them on a call under Done For You, or we ask for delegated access. Tell me which and I will write it.

Method. Every claim was checked against the code that implements it, not against another document.
