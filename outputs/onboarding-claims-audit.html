StudioLAB Growth, onboarding form

What the form promises, and what actually happens

An accuracy sweep of every claim the onboarding form makes to a studio, checked against what the code, the database and the post-payment surfaces actually do. All eleven live defects are fixed and in production. Three unreachable ones were deliberately left, with the reason on each. Every defect here was friendly, well written, and would have survived a tone pass untouched.

4 Were untrue All four rewritten. Two also changed what we collect
6 Contradicted us One duration, one turnaround, one story now
4 Unreachable One documented, three left with a stated reason
11 Fixed and live Verified on production, not just merged

Swept: the six live routes (au and us × Launch, Scale, Dominate AI), account.html, js/form.js, and the save-draft, save-kb, scrape-and-extract and cleanup-attachments functions. Not swept: kb.html, the admin console, and outbound email templates, all of which make claims to studios.

Untrue

4 findings

A studio reads these and forms a false expectation. Two of them also change what data we collect, which makes them more than a copy problem.

U1

We promise to handle email DNS, which we cannot do without access to their registrar

All six routes, step 3, two separate places each. 12 instances.

Shipped

What the studio reads

Sending from your own domain helps your emails land in the inbox instead of the spam folder. If you would like this, just tell us the domain and we set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for you during onboarding. There is nothing technical for you to do.

Just the domain, not a full URL. We handle all the technical setup during onboarding, so you will not need to touch any DNS records yourself.

au/scale/index.html lines 527 and 540, mirrored on all six routes

What is actually so

SPF, DKIM and DMARC are records on the studio's own domain, held at their registrar. We can generate the values, but somebody with access to that DNS has to publish them. We do not have that access and the form never asks for it.

The form even has a dns_access question, so the product already knows this varies by studio. The copy promises the outcome anyway.

The second cost is worse than the false promise. A studio who has no idea how to reach their DNS is exactly the studio who should be steered to Done For You. This line tells them there is nothing to worry about, so that signal never fires.

Shipped

The card now reads: we work out the exact records, adding them changes settings on your domain so it needs someone with DNS access, we do it with you on a call under Done-For-You, and send you the records and a walkthrough on Guided. Live on all six routes.

U2

We require the last four of a US owner's social security number on a plan that has no texting

us/launch. Verified live in the browser: the field renders and carries data-required.

Shipped

What the studio reads

Last 4 of owner SSN
Required by US carriers to register a Sole Proprietor SMS sender. We never see the full number.

Shown on us/launch as soon as a studio selects Sole Proprietor, under a heading that says these details are optional

What is actually so

Launch has no SMS. There is no carrier registration, no texting number, and nothing for those digits to be used for.

applyBusinessTypeConditionals() in js/form.js decides visibility from country and business type only. It never checks the plan. So the field appears on Launch exactly as it does on Scale, and because it also carries data-required, a US sole proprietor cannot get past step 2 without entering it.

This is a data question before it is a copy question: we would be collecting sensitive identity data for a stated purpose that does not exist on that plan, and refusing to continue without it.

Shipped

Gone entirely, not plan-gated. Checked the platform first: a US sole proprietor proves identity through the A2P brand check, a third-party ID verification they complete inside their own sub-account, in their own browser. We cannot run it for them or feed digits into it. Removed from the routes, the payload, the hydrate map, the admin view, and from save-draft’s allow-list so nothing can write it again. No production row ever held one.

U3

The consent tick box asks Launch studios to authorise text messages we will never send

au/launch and us/launch, step 4. This is the box that blocks submission.

Shipped

What the studio reads

I authorise StudioLAB Growth to send emails and text messages to my families on my studio's behalf, using the studio name, branding, and contact details I have provided, for the automations and communications included in my plan.

Identical wording on all six routes, including both Launch routes

What is actually so

Launch is email automations only. The authorisation collected is broader than the service bought.

The clause does end with "included in my plan", which limits it, but the studio is still being asked to tick a permission for a channel they have not purchased. On a page whose whole job is to be trustworthy about what happens to their families' contact details, that reads badly.

Elsewhere the form already handles this correctly. The business details intro says the tax details matter "on text-enabled plans", which is exactly the right hedge. The consent line simply was not given the same treatment.

Shipped

Both Launch routes now read "send emails to my families". Gary’s call: revisit if they upgrade.

au/scale and au/ai, step 4. The US routes add "(A2P / 10DLC)" but keep the same promise.

Contradictory

6 findings

Nothing here is a lie on its own. Each one is two StudioLAB surfaces telling the same studio different things, usually minutes apart.

C1

Every route disagrees with itself about how long the form takes

All six routes. The two numbers sit one screen apart.

Shipped

The two screens, in order

Launch
Welcome door: About 5 minutes
Step 1 card: Takes about 10 minutes

Scale
Welcome door: About 10 minutes
Step 1 card: Takes about 15 minutes

Dominate AI
Welcome door: About 15 minutes
Step 1 card: This form takes about 10 minutes

What is actually so

There is one coded source of truth: PLAN_MINS in js/setup-gate.js holds 5, 10 and 15. The old auth gate used it, and the new welcome door inherited it. The step 1 cards are hand written text that nobody reconciled.

Dominate AI is the one that stings. The door says 15 minutes and the very next screen says 10, so the longest plan advertises itself as the quickest. It also happens to be the plan with the highest setup fee.

Shipped

One line, one number, every plan: "about 10 minutes, and it saves as you go". Generated from a single constant in the door script, so the door and step 1 cannot drift apart again.

C2

Three surfaces quote four different turnaround times for the same setup

Step 1, the setup cards at step 5, and account.html after payment.

Shipped

What a Scale studio reads, in sequence

Step 1
Your account is typically ready in 3 to 7 business days

Step 5, the card they pay on
Done For You: Typically 5 to 7 business days
Guided: Typically 3 to 5 business days

account.html, right after paying
Reviewing your submission and setting up your account behind the scenes. This typically takes 1 to 2 business days

What is actually so

The setup card text is the one generated from code (SETUP_DISPLAY in js/form.js), and it is the number attached to the price they agree to, so it is the one that counts commercially.

The account page then quotes 1 to 2 days to the same studio, which is both the lowest number and the last one they read. Beating it looks impossible; missing it looks like a broken promise on the surface they will check repeatedly.

Dominate AI is consistent, at 7 to 10 days in both places. Only the Launch and Scale branch is wrong.

Shipped

Gary’s call: keep it general. "Most studios are live within 3 to 5 business days", 7 to 10 on Dominate AI, identical on step 1, the setup cards and the account page. Timings came off the setup cards entirely, since putting them there is what created four numbers in the first place.

C3

We tell Scale studios to prepare their lead sources, then never ask for them

Scale and Dominate AI. Step 1 promises it, step 4 is titled after it, no input exists.

Shipped

What the studio reads

Step 1, what you'll need before you start
Which lead sources to connect: Facebook Lead Ads, Google Business Messages, website chat

Step 4 heading and intro
Text messaging and lead sources
Optional setup for SMS automations and lead-source tracking on your Scale plan.

What is actually so

Step 4 collects one thing: a Google Business Profile URL. There is no lead source picker anywhere in any route.

So a studio does homework we asked for, arrives ready, is never asked, and we start their build without the answer.

C5

The US routes are written in Australian English

us/launch, us/scale, us/ai. Nine visible instances per route.

What a US studio reads

Brand colour hex codes, or a screenshot we can match

Brand colours  ·  Primary colour  ·  Secondary colour

Please choose a primary colour.

Drop a screenshot or website image here and we will match the colours for you.

What is actually so

There is a runtime spelling swap, swapSpelling(), and it looked like this was handled. It only converts the enrol and enrolment family. Colour, authorise, organise and the rest are untouched.

C6

Both regions are offered the other country's legal entity types

All six routes, step 2, on the field that feeds tax and carrier registration.

Shipped

The same list on every route

Sole Proprietor  ·  LLC  ·  Corporation  ·  Partnership  ·  Non-profit  ·  Pty Ltd (Australian company)  ·  Other Australian entity  ·  Other

Not filtered by region, so an Australian studio is offered LLC and a US studio is offered Pty Ltd

What is actually so

LLC is not an Australian structure and Pty Ltd is not a US one. An Australian owner scanning that list can reasonably pick LLC, thinking it is the generic company option.

The ABN and EIN fields underneath are driven by country rather than by this answer, so nothing breaks. But the stored business_type is then wrong on a record that feeds invoices and SMS sender registration.

Shipped

Unreachable

4 findings

No studio can see any of this today, so none of it is urgent. It matters because it makes the codebase lie to whoever reads it next, including me. I nearly filed the first one as a live contradiction before tracing who actually calls it.

D1

A finished success screen that only a spam bot has ever seen

The doneScreen block in all six routes.

Shipped

What the file says a studio sees

All received. Thank you!
We've got everything we need to get your Scale setup underway. Our team will review your submission and be in touch shortly.

Timeline   Typically 3 to 5 business days

What is actually so

Two functions can reveal that panel. showDone() is called from exactly one place, the honeypot branch that fires when the hidden spam trap field is filled. showSavedForLater() overwrites the heading, the description and the timeline with "Draft saved. Pay when you're ready."

So the written copy reaches nobody. A real studio goes from step 5 to Stripe and comes back to the account page.

The risk is not today, it is the next person. Anyone reading these files reasonably believes studios see this, and the timelines in it disagree with the setup cards. The Dominate AI version quotes 5 to 10 days against a card that says 7 to 10.

Shipped

Left in place with a comment naming the only two functions that reach it and noting that both rewrite the text. Its timelines were aligned with the new numbers anyway, so a future reader is not comparing a stale figure against a live one.

What shipped, and one thing that came out of it

4 passes

Route edits ran through a script that asserts a hit count per replacement and is idempotent, so a missed match failed loudly instead of quietly doing nothing. All four passes are live and verified against production, not just merged.

1

The two that changed what we hold

The SSN field is gone from the product, and Launch no longer collects authorisation for text messages it cannot send. The SSN decision came out of reading the platform's own A2P flow rather than reasoning about it: identity is proved by a third-party check the owner completes themselves, so the digits were never going anywhere.

2

The two promises we could not keep

Custom domains and text messaging now say what we do and what only they can do. Both previously hid the signal that should route an unconfident studio toward the paid setup, so honest copy here should help the Done-For-You case rather than hurt it.

3

Done-For-You is no longer sold as hands-off

Gary's steer, and the biggest single change. The setup step now names what only they can do, whichever path they pick: social logins, domain records, carrier verification. The difference between the two options is how much of the rest we do and whether we are on the call, not whether they touch it at all.

4

Numbers, region, and the follow-up nobody was sending

One duration and one turnaround everywhere. US routes actually read as US English now. And a new nightly job chases studios who start setup and stop: three emails at roughly day 3, day 7 and day 14, then it leaves them alone.

Two things the follow-up job exposed

Both fixed

Neither was in the audit. Both were found by dry-running the new cron before trusting it, which is the whole argument for dry runs.

X1

Two nightly jobs would have emailed real studios from test mode

nudge-abandoned-kb and nudge-setup-tasks.

Shipped

The rule

While Stripe is in test mode, any side-effect email redirects to the test inbox instead of the real recipient.

What was actually happening

Both called Mailgun directly and skipped the gate, so a test-mode run would have gone to live inboxes. quote-reminders was fine; it hand-rolls the same logic inline instead of importing the shared helper, which is why the gap was easy to miss.

Shipped

Both now use the shared gated sender, along with the new follow-up job.

X2

Redeploying a cron function silently broke its nightly job

All five cron-invoked functions.

Shipped

What it looked like

Deployed Functions on project: nudge-abandoned-kb

A clean deploy, no warning, no error

What is actually so

These functions had been deployed with a command-line flag that turns off the gateway's JWT check, because they authenticate with a shared secret that is not a JWT. That flag lives nowhere in the repo, so a plain redeploy drops it and the gateway starts rejecting the nightly call before the function ever runs.

It would have failed silently every night. The job fires, gets a 401, and nothing surfaces.

Shipped

The setting now lives in supabase/config.toml, so a plain deploy keeps it. Confirmed all four registered jobs authenticate the same way, so this is the correct setting rather than a widening, and each function still rejects a bad token on its own.

Still open

2 questions, 1 dependency

The three questions from the first pass are answered and built. These are what is left.

Does StudioLAB Growth send its own email at signup, and how many?

This gates the whole cutover. If the platform sends one and we send ours, a studio gets two emails carrying two different links, which is the main way this goes wrong in production.

Does the AI knowledge base read the StudioLAB database automatically now?

If it does, the knowledge base page shrinks to a single question. If it does not, keep it as is: our capture is the source, and the export exists precisely to be pasted into the platform.

The follow-up job only reaches studios who opened the form

A studio who signed up and never opened it leaves no record on our side at all. The only trace is the signup webhook receiver in the Connector, which is built and green but not deployed. Deploy that and the second half of the follow-up can go in.

Method. Every claim was checked against the code that implements it, not against another document. Where a finding turned on whether a studio can actually reach a screen, the callers were traced rather than assumed, which is how the four unreachable items were separated from the live ones and why the "untrue" count is four rather than six. The platform-side research on A2P was done before deciding on the SSN, not after. Not covered: kb.html, the admin console, and the outbound email templates, all of which make claims to studios and none of which were swept. Voice was deliberately ignored, and is the next pass: the door and step 1 still both open with "Welcome to StudioLAB Growth". None of it mattered until the sentences were true.
